01 Context & Phase 1 Goal
ℹ Phase 0 is complete — a functional app with first-cut UX exists at rasa-app-woad.vercel.app. Phase 1 is not a rebuild. It is a focused redesign of user flows and experiences based on a thorough product brainstorm. Work from the existing design system and component library where possible.
What Rasa is

Rasa is a home cooking companion — not a meal planning app, not a recipe database. It understands your lifestyle, your family, your fridge, and your actual week, then makes the entire loop from planning to shopping to cooking to feeling proud feel effortless and warm. Healthy by default, without any tracking.

Phase 1 north star

The founder (sole Phase 1 user) goes from a fridge snapshot to a delightful, personalised meal plan to actual meals cooked — in under 5 minutes of total app interaction per week. Every design decision should be tested against: "does this make that journey shorter, clearer, or more delightful?"

User context

Single user, desktop-first, household with family. Feedback prompts shown in-app (no push notifications in Phase 1). The founder is not a beginner cook but finds meal planning mentally exhausting. The app should feel like relief, not another task.

02 Tone of Voice
✦ Every word in the app — labels, empty states, confirmations, errors, tips, prompts — should sound like a trusted friend who happens to know a lot about cooking. Warm, direct, occasionally funny. Never clinical. Never corporate.
Never write like this
Please select your dietary restrictions from the options below.
Anything we should never put on the menu? Allergies, things you'd rather forget exist?
Error: Inventory update failed. Please try again.
Hmm, something went wrong saving that. Give it one more try?
Meal plan generated successfully. 7 meals added.
Your week is sorted. 7 dinners, zero decision fatigue.
No recipes found. Add recipes to your recipe bank to get started.
Your recipe bank is empty for now — it fills up the moment you generate your first plan.
Rules
✓ Use contractions. "You're" not "You are."
✓ Humour is welcome — when it fits naturally. Never forced.
✓ Celebrate small wins. Cooking is hard — acknowledge it warmly.
✓ Short sentences. One idea at a time.
✓ Use "pantry" not "inventory." "Meals" not "meal entities."
✕ No guilt. Never imply she didn't cook enough or plan poorly.
✕ No passive voice. Active and direct always.
✕ Never write "successfully." If it worked, just say what happened.
✕ No sarcasm or "clever" copy that could land wrong.

05 Onboarding — 3 Questions, Then Immediate Value
⚠ Core principle: Never make her feel like she's filling out a form before she gets value. These 3 questions are the absolute minimum needed to not fail. After question 3, she is taken directly to the pantry input screen and generates her first plan immediately. Everything else surfaces gradually — through a Profile screen she explores when ready, or inferred automatically from behaviour over time.
Onboarding — Screen 1 of 3 /onboarding/1
Question

"Any dietary rules or allergies we must know about?"

Input type

Free text field. Also offer quick-tap chips below for common cases: Vegetarian · Vegan · Halal · Gluten-free · Dairy-free. Chips populate the text field, user can still edit freely.

Why first

A plan that violates a dietary rule or allergy is worse than no plan. This question cannot be skipped — but it can be left blank ("None") if nothing applies.

✓ "None / no restrictions" is a clearly available option — skip is not forced-blank
✓ Quick-tap chips for most common cases to reduce typing
✓ Progress indicator visible (1 of 3) — she knows how long this is
✓ Tone: warm, not clinical. Not "Select dietary restrictions." See tone section.

06 Home / Today View
ℹ The existing Home screen shows today's planned dishes, a cooking session card, and quick actions — good bones. This section refines and tightens that experience, including renaming the cooking session card (see below).
Home /
"What's Cooking" card — primary hero

Naming note: Previously "Tonight's Kitchen" — renamed because cooking sessions happen at any time of day. Breakfast at 7am, lunch, dinner — the card must work for all of them. "What's Cooking" is warm, timeless, and carries no time-of-day assumption.

This is the most important element on the screen. When a meal is planned, this card dominates. It shows: meal name, estimated cook time, a prep-ahead note if relevant ("Marinate the chicken now if you have 5 minutes"), and two CTAs — one to enter the step-by-step cook mode, one to view the full recipe detail.

"View full recipe" opens the recipe detail page. These are complementary, not competing. Cook mode is for active kitchen use; recipe detail is for reading ahead, checking ingredients, or referencing the prep-ahead notes.
✓ Card is the visual anchor of the screen — not just another item in a list
✓ "Let's cook" → launches existing step-by-step cook mode. Do not remove or replace.
✓ "View full recipe" → opens recipe detail page. Secondary CTA, visually lighter than "Let's cook"

Compact horizontal scroll or mini-calendar showing which days have meals planned this week. Tapping a day's meal should navigate to that recipe. Days without meals should be visually distinct but not alarming — not every night needs a plan.

✓ Empty days look intentional, not like broken UI

✓ Prompt appears as an in-context card, not a modal or alert

Each planned meal card displays: meal name, cook time, a one-line reasoning note (subtle, secondary typography — e.g. "Uses your spinach before it expires"), and action affordances (swap, mark as cooked). Cooked meals show their feedback rating if given.

✓ Reasoning note is visually subordinate — supporting info, not headline
✓ Lock toggles are fully removed — do not appear anywhere
✓ "Mark as cooked" action is accessible directly from the meal card
✓ Cooked state is visually distinct — a checked/done indicator, not a deletion
✓ Meal feedback rating shows on card if given (e.g. a small "loved it" indicator)

User taps "Swap"
A reason prompt appears — compact, below or adjacent to the card. Not a modal if avoidable.
2
Reason selection (3 options)
Tap to select one reason. Each reason drives different replacement logic in the AI.
🧅 Missing an ingredient
⏱ No time tonight
✏️ Something else
"Something else" opens a short free-text field. "Missing an ingredient" prompts "Which one?" — optional — to refine alternatives.
3

08 Plan Generation Flow
✦ This is the most important flow in the app. It happens every week and is the moment she gets value. It should feel like a conversation with a thoughtful friend, not a form submission.
Plan Generation /planner/generate
1
Pantry input — free text, no units required
Large, comfortable text area. Prompt copy: "What's in your fridge and pantry right now?" Sub-copy: "Just list what you have — no need for amounts." She types or pastes a comma-separated list, or writes naturally.
"chicken thighs, pasta, canned tomatoes, garlic, spinach, eggs, half a block of paneer"
If quantities are missing for some items, that's fine — the AI handles it gracefully.
2
Week context — optional conversational field
Below the pantry input, a second field. Prompt copy: "Anything about this week we should know?" Sub-copy: "Busy nights, guests coming, want to eat lighter — anything helps." This is optional but strongly encouraged. Shown with a softer visual weight than the pantry field.
"Busy Thursday, parents visiting Saturday. Want to keep it lighter overall this week."
3
Use soon — optional third prompt
A third lightweight field below week context. Prompt copy: "Anything that needs using up soon?" This is where she flags items that aren't necessarily expiring today but shouldn't sit another week — wilting veg, leftovers, things she overbought. AI treats anything listed here as highest priority — slots them into Monday and Tuesday meals before planning anything else. Visually the lightest of the three fields; optional with no pressure.
"Wilting spinach, some sausages from last week, half a block of tofu I need to use."
✓ Separate from the main pantry field — she might not think to mention expiring items when listing what she has
✓ Free text — no structure required. "Sausages" is fine. "Sausages 400g expires Tuesday" is also fine.
✓ AI reasoning note on the plan should reference this: "Monday uses your sausages before they go off"
4
Generation — loading state
A warm, engaged loading experience. Not a spinner on a blank page. Show something that communicates what's happening — "Figuring out how to use that spinach first…" or "Building your week around what you've got…". 10–15 seconds expected.
✓ Loading copy should be conversational and specific to her inputs — not generic
✓ No blank spinner — give her something warm to look at
5
Plan reveal — the wow moment
The plan lands with impact. Each meal shows its name, cook time, and its one-line reasoning note. The transition from loading to plan should feel like something arriving, not just data appearing. After the plan is shown, generated recipes are automatically saved to the recipe bank — a subtle confirmation note: "These recipes have been saved to your bank."
✓ Meal reasoning is visible immediately — not hidden behind an expand
✓ "Shopping list ready" shortcut visible — she can go straight to what to buy
✓ Subtle confirmation that recipes were saved to her bank
✓ Plan reveal should feel like arrival — consider a subtle animation

10 Recipe Detail
ℹ The recipe detail page is where cooking actually happens. It needs to be functional during active cooking — large text, clear step-by-step progression, and embedded tips that feel like a knowledgeable friend whispering at exactly the right moment. Remove all macro/calorie displays.
✦ Step-by-step cook mode — preserve existing implementation. The app already has a carousel-style step-by-step cook mode accessible from the "What's Cooking" card. This is a separate experience from the recipe detail page and must not be removed. Recipe detail = full recipe for reading and reference. Cook mode = focused step-by-step for active kitchen use, one step at a time. Phase 1 work on this screen is additive only: embed cooking tips per step and add the prep-ahead section. The carousel mechanic itself stays untouched.
Recipe Detail /recipes/[id]
Header info

Recipe name, cuisine tag, cook time, serves count. No calorie or macro information — removed entirely.

Measurement toggle — Cups · Grams · Both

A small 3-way toggle in the recipe header, next to the serves count. Switches all ingredient quantities in the recipe — and in cook mode — to the selected system for this session. No global preference needed; she toggles per recipe as she sees fit.

ℹ Why per-recipe, not global: the same person uses cups for savory cooking and grams for baking. A global setting would be wrong half the time. The toggle is always visible and one tap — no friction.
✓ Default: Both — shows "1½ cups / 300g" so she always has a reference before she's chosen her preference
✓ Toggle state carries through from recipe detail into cook mode for the same recipe — they stay in sync
✓ Applies to ingredient list, step quantities, and cooking tip quantities
✓ No persistence required — resets to Both on next open. She'll flip it in 1 tap if she needs to.
Prep ahead — collapsible section, shown before ingredients

A collapsible card at the top of the recipe, before ingredients. Shows what can be done ahead of time — earlier in the day, or the night before. Example: "Marinate the chicken for at least 1 hour — do this now if you have time." Visible but collapsed by default if nothing time-sensitive; expanded by default if something should be done soon.

✓ Shown before the ingredient list — because prep-ahead prep may change what she does right now
✓ AI generates this for all recipes, including imports
✓ Collapsible — not always the first thing she needs, but visible when relevant
Recipe steps with embedded cooking tips

Each step has its instruction text. Below that, optionally, an embedded tip — a distinct visual treatment (subtle coloured callout, not a modal, not a popup). The tip appears as part of the step, not as a separate tips section.

3
Add onions and cook until golden brown.
⏱ This takes 10–12 minutes on medium — not 3. Don't rush on high heat. The sweetness that makes the dish comes from patient onions. While you wait, clean the chopping board.
4
Add tomatoes, ginger, garlic. Cook until oil separates.
✓ "Oil separates" means you'll see ghee pooling at the edges of the masala — that's your signal, not the clock. Usually 6–8 minutes.
Four tip types — different visual treatments per type
⏱ TIMING REALITY CHECK
"This takes 10–12 min, not 3." Used when recipes understate time.
✓ DONENESS SIGNAL
"Oil separates at the edges — that's your cue." Used for visual/sensory outcomes.
⚠ HEADS UP
"The sauce looks curdled — keep stirring." Prevents panic at alarming-but-normal moments.
✦ CLEAN AS YOU GO
"While you wait, wipe the board." Converts idle time into cleanup — no cleanup feature needed.
"Serve with" — functional accompaniment field
⚠ This applies to manually added and imported recipes only. AI-generated recipes are complete meals by default — protein, carb, and vegetable already included in one recipe with coordinated steps. This field exists for single-dish recipes that arrive via paste or manual entry.

When a recipe is saved (imported or manual), the AI checks whether it constitutes a complete meal — protein + carb + vegetable. If a component is missing, it pre-fills a "Serve with" suggestion. She can accept, edit, or dismiss. This field is functional, not decorative: its ingredients feed into the shopping list and planner display.

How it works end to end
1
At recipe save — AI detects and suggests
After parsing the recipe, AI assesses completeness. If it's a single dish, it pre-fills "Serve with" with a suggestion. One tap to accept, or she edits it freely. Fully dismissable — she might always cook rice from habit and not need the app to track it.
AI pre-fill: "Basmati rice or warm naan" → she edits to "roti" → saves
2
In the planner — full meal shown
When this recipe is included in a week's plan, the meal entry shows both components. The planner entry reads "Dal Tadka + Roti" — not just "Dal Tadka." She can see the complete meal at a glance.
3
On the shopping list — accompaniment included
Accompaniment ingredients are automatically added to the shopping list when this recipe is planned. Grouped under the meal, clearly labelled. If she dismissed "Serve with," nothing is added — that's her choice.
Shopping list: "Dal Tadka + Roti — red lentils, ghee, tomatoes, atta flour"
4
In cook mode — brief note, not a coordinated session
A persistent note at the top of cook mode: "Also make: roti." Not a full parallel recipe track — that's Phase 2. Most accompaniments (rice, bread, simple salads) don't need step-by-step guidance. She sees the reminder, handles it herself.
✓ AI skips the prompt for recipes already complete — a chicken tray-bake with veg included doesn't get a "Serve with" suggestion
✓ "Serve with" is editable after saving — she can update it anytime from the recipe detail page
✓ Dismissing it is valid — no guilt, no re-prompting
✓ Planner and shopping list only include accompaniment when "Serve with" is set — not assumed
Mark as cooked — primary action

"Done cooking" button at the bottom of the recipe. Tapping it marks the meal as cooked in the planner. No feedback prompt here — the family hasn't eaten yet. The prompt surfaces the next time she opens the app.

✓ Prominent — this is a key tap in the product loop
✓ Confirmation copy: "Nice work. Dinner's done." — warm acknowledgement, not just a checkbox
✓ Navigates back to Home after marking cooked

13 Shopping List
