Rasa — AI Plan Engine: Inputs & Generation Logic

PM reference document · Phase 1 · Updated after full product brainstorm session · For UX and ENG

Tone of voice — applies to every word in the app

Every message, label, prompt, empty state, error, confirmation, and tip in Rasa should sound like a trusted friend who happens to know a lot about cooking — warm, direct, occasionally funny, never clinical. Not a corporate app. Not a recipe database. A person in your corner.

Never write like this
"Please select your dietary restrictions from the options below."
"Error: Inventory update failed. Please try again."
"Meal plan generated successfully. 7 meals added."
"No recipes found. Add recipes to your recipe bank to get started."
Write like this instead
"Anything we should never put on the menu? Allergies, dietary rules, ingredients you'd rather forget exist?"
"Hmm, something went wrong saving that. Give it one more try?"
"Your week is sorted. 7 dinners, zero decision fatigue."
"Your recipe bank is empty for now — it fills up the moment you generate your first plan."
Rules
✓ Use contractions. "You're" not "You are."
✓ Humour welcome — when it fits naturally. Never forced.
✓ Celebrate small wins. Cooking is hard work — acknowledge it.
✓ Short sentences. One idea at a time.
✕ No jargon. "Pantry" not "inventory." "Meals" not "meal entities."
✕ No guilt. Never imply she didn't cook enough or plan well enough.
✕ No passive voice. Active and direct always.
✕ Never write "successfully." If it worked, just say what happened.
What drives the plan — all inputs and their priority
Hard constraints — checked first, non-negotiable
Dietary rules & allergies
Vegetarian, halal, gluten-free, nut allergy, shellfish, etc. These are hard filters — a plan violating these is unusable or unsafe.
Never suggest meat to a vegetarian. Never suggest nuts to an allergy household. No second chances.
Critical — set at onboarding
Who are you cooking for?
Family composition shapes the entire recipe set. Young kids = mild spice, familiar proteins, no unusual textures. Just adults = more adventurous.
"Me and partner" vs "family with a 6-year-old" produce completely different plans from the same pantry.
Critical — set at onboarding
Ingredients never to include
Not allergies — just things the household will never eat. Cilantro, mushrooms, fish, liver, blue cheese. Household-specific hard stops.
"Anything you'd never want to see on the menu?" Free text, parsed by AI.
Critical — set at onboarding

Recipe bank
Grows over time as a byproduct of planning — not a prerequisite. Week 1: mostly AI-generated. Week 8: mix of AI and her proven meals. "Make again" rated meals get rotated back in every 3–4 weeks automatically.

Feedback signals New Phase 2
Captured from week 1 but used in plan generation from Phase 2. "Everyone loved it" → weight up. "Won't make again" → retired. Skipped meals → deprioritised. Consistent Friday skips → stop planning Fridays.
Passive signals: skipped, swapped, never cooked. Active: 3-tap family verdict after dinner.

Internal generation rules — invisible to user
Variety engine
No back-to-back same protein. Cuisine mix across the week. Easy–hard rhythm (lighter weekdays, more effort weekends). One-pan and minimal-dishes variants preferred on weeknights to reduce cleanup.
Invisible when it works. Noticeable when it fails (chicken 4 nights in a row).
Reasoning surface
AI explains why each meal was chosen, shown inline on the plan. Builds trust — she feels heard, not processed.
"Monday uses your spinach before it expires." · "Quick 25-min meal for your busy Thursday." · "Weekend treat — a bit more effort, worth it."
Builds trust. "Delight, not magic."
AI Plan Generator — generation order
1. Apply hard constraints (allergies, family composition, banned ingredients)
2. Prioritise pantry items with expiry → surface to Mon/Tue
3. Parse weekly context → map busy nights, occasions, lighter days
4. Match primary cuisine (60–70% of meals) + weave in secondary
5. Apply skill ceiling and weeknight time budget per day
6. Pull from recipe bank if available and relevant
7. Apply variety engine (protein rotation, cuisine mix, effort rhythm)
8. Generate complete-meal recipes — each AI-generated recipe includes protein + carb + vegetable with steps sequenced to coordinate timing. If using a user's single-dish recipe from the bank, include its "Serve with" accompaniment in the plan entry and shopping list.
9. Generate reasoning per meal
10. Auto-save generated recipes to recipe bank

Recipe completeness model — AI-generated vs user-added
AI-generated recipes
Always complete meals — protein + carb + vegetable, all components included in one recipe with steps sequenced to coordinate timing. "Dal Tadka with Basmati Rice and Cucumber Raita" not just "Dal Tadka." Shopping list is automatically complete.
User-imported or manually added recipes
Likely single-dish. AI detects completeness at save time and suggests a "Serve with" accompaniment — editable, dismissable. When planned, the accompaniment ingredients are added to the shopping list. Planner shows "[Recipe] + [Accompaniment]". Cook mode shows a brief "Also make: X" note.
Multi-recipe meal coordination (Phase 2): Proper meal bundles with multiple recipe objects, parallel cook mode with coordinated timings, and reusable component recipes (e.g. "basmati rice" as a standalone recipe used across many meals). Not built in Phase 1 — the "Serve with" model covers the need adequately for now.
First-run onboarding — 3 questions, then immediate value

Core principle: Never make her feel like she's filling out a form before she gets value. We ask 3 questions — the minimum needed to not fail — then generate the first plan immediately. Everything else surfaces gradually through use or an optional profile screen. Value first, depth optional.

1
Any dietary rules or allergies we must know about?
Free text. This is the one question that cannot be skipped — a plan that violates a dietary rule or allergy is worse than no plan. Collected first because failure here is catastrophic, not just suboptimal.
"Vegetarian" · "Halal" · "My daughter is allergic to nuts" · "We don't eat pork"
2
Who are you cooking for?
Single select. Determines spice tolerance, portion sizing, recipe complexity, and texture constraints across every plan. A plan built for adults that lands on a 6-year-old's plate is the wrong plan — regardless of cuisine.
Just me · Me and a partner · Family with young kids (under 10) · Family with teens
3
What's your primary cuisine — and what else do you enjoy?
One primary (the cuisine she eats 60–70% of the time) + optional secondary taps. Fast — 10 seconds. Not a flat multi-select: hierarchy matters to the plan generator. Primary anchors the week; secondary weaves in for variety.
Primary: Indian · Secondary (optional taps): Italian, Mediterranean
→
Immediately: pantry snapshot + week context → first plan generated
After question 3, she lands on the pantry input screen. Types what she has. Optionally types a sentence about her week. Plan appears in under 15 seconds. Recipe bank starts with 5–7 entries. The wow moment arrives before she's even thought about exploring the rest of the app.

Weekly plan generation — what happens every Sunday (or whenever she re-plans)
1
Pantry snapshot — free text, no units required
She types what she has. Unstructured. If units/quantities are missing, AI treats the item as "available, unknown quantity." Planning still proceeds — the AI avoids recipes that depend on precise measurements of unlabelled items but uses the ingredient freely otherwise.
"spinach, 2 chicken breasts, eggs, pasta, garlic, half a block of paneer, canned chickpeas"
2
This week's context — conversational free text
One open field below the pantry input. "Tell me what your week looks like." Not required but strongly encouraged. AI extracts busy nights, occasion meals, energy levels, and any one-off constraints. Her profile handles everything else automatically.
"Normal week, but Thursday I'll be home late. Want something lighter overall — been eating heavy."
3
Use soon — optional, highest AI priority
A third lightweight field. "Anything that needs using up soon?" Free text. AI treats anything listed here as the highest priority in the plan — slots these ingredients into Monday and Tuesday meals before planning anything else. Reasoning note on the plan should reference it explicitly: "Monday uses your sausages before they go off."
"Wilting spinach, some sausages from last week, half a block of tofu."
Shopping list — what it is and what it isn't

The real question isn't "how do we sync the loop?" — it's "do we need a persistent fridge database at all?"

Why persistent inventory is a trap
✕ Every item she uses requires a manual update
✕ Every item she buys but doesn't check off → inventory wrong
✕ Quantities are ambiguous ("bought chicken" = 4 portions or 1 pack?)
✕ Stale data = bad plan suggestions. Requires constant maintenance for any accuracy.
The simpler model that works better
✓ Pantry snapshot at plan generation = always fresh, zero maintenance
✓ Shopping list = plan ingredients minus pantry snapshot. Always accurate.
✓ Checking off items in-store = for her use only. No back-sync required.
✓ Mid-week regeneration uses current pantry — she types what she has again. Fresh, not stale.
Verdict: Fridge page → "This week's pantry" · session input only · becomes plan generation trigger Shopping list → one-way output, no sync Persistent inventory DB → deleted · no real data · not rebuilt
Explicit non-goals — what this engine does not do
✕ Track macros or calories — healthy by default, no numbers shown
✕ Require units/quantities on pantry items to generate a plan
✕ Maintain a persistent inventory that the user must keep up to date
✕ Require a recipe bank before generating the first plan
✕ Regenerate the full week when she swaps one meal
✕ Ask the same onboarding questions again after day 1
✕ Silently change the plan without her knowing (mid-week adjustment = Phase 2, with prompt)
✕ Generate 7 meals if her lifestyle suggests she cooks 4–5 nights
